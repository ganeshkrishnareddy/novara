Treatments

Care organised around your child's needs

Eighteen illustrative service entries, grouped so you can scan by purpose. Descriptions are general prototype copy, not clinical advice.

Preventive & routine

  • First visit

    A short, friendly hello to the chair, the mirror and the team.

    What to expect: a look, a chat and a simple home plan.

  • Check-up & clean

    A gentle review of teeth and gums with a tidy-up where needed.

    What to expect: a clear summary you can take home.

  • Fluoride varnish

    A quick protective coating for teeth that need extra help.

    What to expect: paint-on, brief setting time, no drilling.

  • Sealants

    A smooth shield over the grooves at the back of little molars.

    What to expect: cleaning, drying, then a thin resin layer.

Restorative

  • Tooth-coloured filling

    A small, neat repair that blends in with the surrounding tooth.

    What to expect: numbing if needed, then a shade-matched fill.

  • Protective crown

    A cap that guards a baby tooth after larger repair work.

    What to expect: fitting and cementing in one visit.

  • Tooth-shaped crown

    A more natural-looking option for visible teeth when appropriate.

    What to expect: planning, preparation and a fitted final crown.

  • Space maintainer

    A small device that keeps room for a tooth still developing.

    What to expect: measurements, fitting and review visits.

Anxiety & sedation

  • Calm visit plan

    A step-by-step plan with pauses, signals and plenty of preparation.

    What to expect: a conversation before any treatment.

  • Nitrous oxide

    An inhaled option sometimes used to take the edge off a visit.

    What to expect: a suitability discussion and monitored use.

  • Oral sedation

    A prescribed medicine option for children who need more support.

    What to expect: pre-visit instructions and an escort home.

  • General anaesthesia

    A hospital-style option for extensive treatment when clinically indicated.

    What to expect: a separate assessment and care pathway.

Orthodontic & alignment

  • Early alignment check

    A look at growth patterns and habits while teeth are still moving.

    What to expect: observation first, referral only if useful.

  • Myobrace-style trainer

    A removable appliance concept sometimes used in early guidance.

    What to expect: a fitting and a home-wear routine.

  • Clear aligner guidance

    Information about transparent aligners for older children and teens.

    What to expect: an assessment and a staged plan.

Surgical & urgent care

  • Tongue-tie assessment

    A conversation about feeding, speech or movement concerns.

    What to expect: assessment, then a gentle recommendation.

  • Lip-tie assessment

    A review of how the lip moves and whether it causes difficulty.

    What to expect: assessment and clear next steps.

  • Urgent tooth care

    Advice for a bump, a lost filling or a sudden toothache.

    What to expect: call a real dental service; this site is not live.

Service names are deliberately consistent across the page and the enquiry form. Illustrative entries only — no service is actually offered.

Privacy & consent

Sample privacy notice

This section stands in for a real privacy page. It is illustrative and contains no legal commitment.

What happens in this prototype: nothing is transmitted, nothing is stored, no analytics run, no cookies are set and no third-party scripts load. Form values remain only in the current browser form until you close or reload the page.

What a real service should explain: why data is collected, who sees it, how long it is kept, how a parent withdraws consent, and how to contact the clinic. None of those processes exist here.

Consent controls: the notice near the top of the page has an essential control that is permanently on and an analytics control that toggles a label only. The state is not remembered and nothing is tracked.

Placeholders: hello@example.test and +971 00 000 0000 are fictional contact details. No real email address or phone number is used anywhere in this prototype.
